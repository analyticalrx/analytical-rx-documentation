AnalyticalRx Phase 2 Product Design

Workflow and automation design for the phase after the Phase 1 starter dashboards and ingestion foundation are live.

Draft created July 26, 2026. This file intentionally lives outside the main PRD so Phase 2 can be refined without disturbing the Phase 1 PRD commitment.

Scope Note

Phase boundary: Phase 1 includes starter dashboards for all roles. Those dashboards are intentionally simple but final as a Phase 1 commitment. Phase 2 should not be framed as the first time role dashboards exist. Phase 2 improves the dashboards by adding workflow execution, automation output, doctor CRM activity, refill queues, reminders, and operational follow-through.

This design is a working proposal. If Phase 1 data availability, PrimeRx field coverage, or client workflow details are not clear enough, unresolved items should stay marked as assumptions or deferred instead of forcing a premature requirement.

1. Phase 2 Objective

Phase 2 should move AnalyticalRx from visibility to execution. Phase 1 shows users what is happening. Phase 2 should help users act on it by preparing daily work queues, scheduling follow-ups, triggering reminders, and capturing outcomes.

The main Phase 2 product theme is: the system should prepare the work before the user has to search for it.

Doctor Growth

Manage doctor outreach, visits, pitches, follow-ups, and outcomes in one workflow-driven CRM.

Prepared Work Queues

Automatically surface daily and upcoming refill work for pharmacists and technicians.

Workflow Automation

Introduce predefined background workflows that create tasks, alerts, schedules, and optional communications.

2. Product Principles

  • Do not rebuild generic Airtable: build pharmacy-specific workflows that came from Airtable intent and client operations.
  • Predefined workflows first: start with a few high-value workflows instead of a fully configurable automation builder.
  • Human verification remains important: automations should prepare work and suggest next steps, but pharmacy staff should verify clinical/operational actions.
  • Role-based work: administrators decide which pharmacists, technicians, managers, and business development users can access each workflow.
  • Email is optional: internal scheduling is baseline; outbound email sending can be enabled only when the customer/product owner approves the workflow, content, and sender rules.
  • Traceable automation: every automated task, alert, email, or queue item should have a clear source, status, and audit trail.

4. Doctor CRM

Phase 2 should include a lightweight doctor relationship CRM focused on pharmacy growth. The goal is not only to store doctor profiles. The product should help the business development team manage outreach, remember what worked, and schedule the next action.

Doctor CRM Requirements

  • Store doctor office profile: office name, provider names, specialty, address, phone/fax/email where allowed, assigned relationship owner, store relationship, and status.
  • Track relationship stage: cold, warm, active, high-value, inactive, do-not-prioritize, or client-approved equivalent.
  • Record visit history: visit date, visitor, purpose, notes, pitch used, objections, outcome, follow-up date, and next action.
  • Capture pitch history: what was presented, which materials were used, what message worked, what did not work, and what the office asked for.
  • Schedule future visits, calls, or email follow-ups inside the AnalyticalRx scheduling system.
  • Create follow-up tasks for business development users, store managers, or owners.
  • Show outreach status and overdue follow-ups on the business development dashboard.
  • Connect doctor activity to prescription volume or revenue only when data quality supports that relationship.

6. Daily Refill Workflow

The daily refill workflow is the clearest Phase 2 pharmacy operations automation. Instead of manually checking PrimeRx or reports for the day’s refill work, the system prepares a queue for the pharmacist and technician.

User Story

When a pharmacist or technician logs in for the day, they should see the refills that need attention today and in the next few days. They should be able to verify, prepare, mark ready, block, or skip the item without searching manually through source reports.

Queue Scope

  • Show refills due today.
  • Show upcoming refills for the next few days. Initial assumption: support 3-day and 7-day views, with the exact default confirmed after Phase 1 data review.
  • Separate queue items by store, assigned role, status, and priority.
  • Show enough context for staff to act without exposing unnecessary patient identifiers in the AnalyticalRx dashboard.
  • Allow pharmacists and technicians to mark status updates based on permitted role access.

Suggested Queue Statuses

StatusMeaningWho Can Update
SuggestedAutomation identified the refill as due or upcoming.System.
VerifiedStaff reviewed the item and confirmed it should be prepared.Pharmacist, authorized technician.
PreparingMedication preparation is in progress.Pharmacist, technician.
ReadyMedication is ready for pickup, delivery, or next operational step.Pharmacist, authorized technician.
BlockedItem cannot proceed because of issue such as rejection, inventory, prior authorization, or missing information.Pharmacist, technician, manager.
CompletedWork item is finished.Pharmacist, technician, system if confirmed by source event.
SkippedItem is intentionally not being worked.Pharmacist, manager.

Refill Workflow Product Rules

  • The queue should be prepared automatically by a scheduled workflow before users start their day.
  • The queue should be refreshable during the day if new data arrives.
  • Users should be able to filter by today, upcoming, blocked, ready, assigned to me, and store.
  • Blocked items should create alerts or manager-visible exceptions.
  • Completed work should improve dashboard visibility into preparation progress and operational workload.

7. Alerts And Emails

Phase 2 should include alerts as part of the automation component. Alerts should be useful, role-specific, and tied to workflow status.

Alert Types

  • New refill queue prepared for the day.
  • Upcoming refills that need verification.
  • Blocked refill items requiring manager or pharmacist attention.
  • Doctor follow-up due today or overdue.
  • Scheduled doctor visit or call reminder.
  • Scheduled doctor email ready to review or send.
  • Compliance renewal due or overdue.
  • Task overdue or blocked.
  • Workflow failure, skipped records, or missing source data.

Email Policy

11. Out Of Scope

  • Fully customizable customer-built automation rule engine.
  • Advanced predictive analytics, forecasting, recommendation engine, or scenario planner.
  • Automatic clinical decision-making or medication changes.
  • Unapproved outbound doctor email automation.
  • Broad patient-level CRM inside AnalyticalRx.
  • Replacing PrimeRx as the system of record for prescriptions, clinical verification, or dispensing workflow.
  • External SaaS billing and broad multi-customer commercialization features.

12. Open Questions

Product User Confirmation

  • Should scheduled doctor emails be sent automatically, or should Phase 2 start with email drafts and reminders only?
  • Who approves doctor outreach templates and sender identity?
  • Should doctor email activity be measured as part of outreach outcomes?
  • What are the first three doctor pitch categories or campaigns the product should support?

Refill Workflow Confirmation

  • What exact upcoming refill window should be default: 3 days, 7 days, or another business rule?
  • Which refill fields can appear in AnalyticalRx without violating the HIPAA-minimized design?
  • Which refill queue statuses require pharmacist-only permission?
  • Should the queue refresh once daily, several times per day, or on every relevant source event?

Automation Confirmation

  • Which predefined workflows should be delivered first if Phase 2 must be split into smaller releases?
  • Which alerts should be in-app only, and which should also send email?
  • Which workflows require manager escalation?
  • Should admins be able to change workflow timing, thresholds, and role access in Phase 2?

13. Acceptance Criteria

Phase 2 Baseline Acceptance

  • All Phase 1 role dashboards remain available and are enhanced, not replaced.
  • Admins can grant or restrict access to workflow views by role, user, and store.
  • Doctor CRM supports office profiles, visit history, pitch history, follow-up scheduling, relationship status, and outcome notes.
  • Business development users can see upcoming doctor visits, follow-ups, overdue outreach, and prior pitch context.
  • The automation component can run predefined scheduled workflows and record workflow audit status.
  • The daily refill workflow prepares today’s refill queue and an upcoming refill queue before users manually search for it.
  • Pharmacists and authorized technicians can update refill queue statuses according to role permissions.
  • Automations can create in-app alerts and internal email alerts where enabled.
  • Doctor email sending is disabled by default and can be enabled only through approved admin settings.
  • Workflow failures, skipped records, and missing data are visible to authorized admin/support users.

Phase 2 Success Measures

  • Pharmacists and technicians spend less time manually identifying refill work.
  • Store managers can see refill preparation progress and blocked items earlier in the day.
  • Business development users have a clear schedule of doctor follow-ups and can review prior pitch context before visits.
  • Owners can see whether workflow execution is improving operational follow-through.
  • Admins can enable predefined automations without engineering intervention for every setting change.
