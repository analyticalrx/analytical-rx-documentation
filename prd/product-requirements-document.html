How To Read This PRD

This PRD starts with the business problem, then explains the product goals, then walks through the delivery phases. Phase 1 and Phase 2 are written as delivery sections because those are the areas we understand well enough to plan today.

Current status: Phase 1 is in progress and includes simple starter dashboards for every role. Phase 2 is planned and focuses on workflows, automations, doctor CRM, refill queues, alerts, and improved dashboards.

Reference material such as data sources, product principles, assumptions, privacy notes, and open questions is moved into appendices so the main PRD reads as a product plan rather than a collection of notes.

2. Problem Statement

Independent multi-pharmacy operators need a reliable way to understand performance across locations and act on operational issues before they become revenue, reimbursement, compliance, or customer-service problems.

Today, the business has useful information spread across pharmacy systems, reports, Airtable, manual workflows, and staff follow-up processes. That makes it hard to quickly answer questions like:

  • Which store is growing, flat, or declining?
  • What is causing the change: prescription volume, payer mix, drug profitability, doctor activity, rejected claims, task follow-through, or another operational issue?
  • What work should each role focus on today?
  • Which doctor relationships are producing results, and what follow-up should happen next?
  • Which refills, documents, tasks, or exceptions need action before they become a problem?

Core product question: Why is each pharmacy growing, staying flat, or declining, and what should the team do next?

3. Background

The client currently uses Airtable as a temporary operating prototype. It captures the intent of the product: store tracking, KPI scorecards, daily sales, financial periods, tasks, compliance items, documents, employee licenses, SOPs, doctor offices, doctor visits, vendors, and role-oriented dashboards.

Airtable is not the target database and should not be copied one-to-one. It is a reference for what users are trying to manage and what workflows matter to the business.

PrimeRx/Micro Merchant is expected to be a primary operational data source. We are planning around webhooks, API access, and SFTP historical/daily feeds. Exact fields, pricing, limits, and setup details still need vendor confirmation.

4. Product Goals

Visibility

Show cross-store and role-specific performance in a clear dashboard experience.

Data Foundation

Ingest PrimeRx/Micro Merchant data into a consistent internal model that can support dashboards and later analytics.

Execution

Prepare daily work queues, follow-ups, reminders, alerts, and workflow tasks so users do not manually search for everything.

Growth Management

Track doctor outreach, pitch history, visit outcomes, referral progress, and future follow-up work.

Operational Control

Track tasks, compliance, documents, licenses, finance close items, and exceptions by store and role.

Future Analytics

Create a foundation for payer analysis, drug profitability, root-cause insights, projections, and SaaS commercialization.

5. Delivery Phases

The delivery plan intentionally starts with data and simple dashboards, then adds workflow execution, then expands into analytics and SaaS productization.

PhaseStatusMain OutcomeWhat Changes For Users
Phase 1 - Data Foundation And Starter Dashboards In progress Build the PrimeRx integration foundation and simple starter dashboards for every role. Users can begin seeing role-level views instead of relying only on manual reports and Airtable-style tracking.
Phase 2 - Workflow Automation And Doctor CRM Planned Add doctor CRM, internal scheduling, daily refill queues, predefined automations, alerts, optional emails, and workflow status. Users move from seeing information to acting on prepared queues, scheduled follow-ups, and automated reminders.
Phase 3 - Analytics And Decision Support Future Add payer/drug analysis, root-cause insights, projections, and recommendations. Owners can see why performance changed and evaluate possible actions using explainable assumptions.

6.4 How Phase 1 Components Work Together

  1. PrimeRx/Micro Merchant sends events or files through webhook, API, or SFTP paths that are approved for Phase 1.
  2. The ingestion layer validates the inbound data, records audit status, and stores restricted raw source details separately from normal dashboard data.
  3. The processing layer maps source data into the canonical event model.
  4. The canonical database connects events to tenant, pharmacy, drug, payer, payment, and audit records where those details are available.
  5. Role-based access determines which stores, dashboards, finance details, compliance details, and restricted records each user can see.
  6. Starter dashboards read from the safe canonical and business tables so users can review early KPIs and operational status.
  7. Import/audit views show admins and developers whether the data pipeline is healthy and what still needs enrichment or correction.

6.5 Phase 1 Out Of Scope

  • Full predictive forecasting and recommendation engine.
  • Final payer contract strategy or guaranteed payer profitability interpretation.
  • Full doctor outreach CRM automation.
  • Full document vault with all compliance workflows.
  • Full configurable automation rule builder.
  • External SaaS billing, customer onboarding, and multi-customer support operations.
  • Any ingestion or display of direct patient identifiers unless specifically approved through compliance review.

6.6 Phase 1 Acceptance Criteria

  • PrimeRx/Micro Merchant data can be received through the agreed ingestion path.
  • Incoming events or files are validated, logged, and processed into the canonical model.
  • The system can identify duplicate, failed, skipped, and retried records.
  • Tenant, pharmacy, drug, payer, payment, and event relationships are stored in a consistent structure.
  • Starter dashboards exist for owner, store manager, pharmacist/technician, bookkeeper, compliance/admin, and business development roles.
  • Restricted raw data is not exposed in normal dashboards.
  • Role-based access rules prevent users from seeing stores, finance, or restricted data outside their authorization.
  • The team can explain what source data is available, what is missing, and what requires API/SFTP enrichment.

7. Phase 2 - Workflow Automation And Doctor CRM

Status: Planned. Phase 2 does not create role dashboards for the first time. Phase 1 already does that. Phase 2 improves those dashboards by adding workflow execution, automation output, doctor CRM activity, refill queues, reminders, alerts, and operational follow-through.

7.1 Phase 2 Outcome

Phase 2 moves AnalyticalRx from visibility to execution. The product should prepare daily work before users have to search for it, then track whether that work was completed, blocked, skipped, or overdue.

7.4 Doctor CRM Detail

  • Store doctor office profile, provider details, specialty, contact information where allowed, assigned relationship owner, store relationship, and status.
  • Track relationship stage such as cold, warm, active, high-value, inactive, do-not-prioritize, or a client-approved equivalent.
  • Record visit history, visit purpose, notes, pitch used, objections, outcome, follow-up date, and next action.
  • Capture pitch history so users can see what worked previously before scheduling the next visit or message.
  • Schedule future visits, calls, internal reminders, and optional doctor email follow-ups.
  • Connect doctor activity to prescription volume or revenue only when data quality supports that relationship.

7.5 Automation Component Detail

  • Start with predefined workflow templates instead of a fully configurable rule builder.
  • Allow admins to enable or disable approved workflows and control which users or roles can access the resulting queues or alerts.
  • Support scheduled workflows, such as daily refill preparation before store opening.
  • Support event-triggered workflows where Phase 1 PrimeRx data quality supports it.
  • Create tasks, queues, in-app alerts, and email alerts where enabled.
  • Maintain audit records for workflow runs, created items, skipped items, failures, and manual overrides.

7.6 Daily Refill Workflow Detail

  • Show refills due today.
  • Show upcoming refills for the next few days. Initial assumption: support 3-day and 7-day views, with the default confirmed after Phase 1 data review.
  • Support pharmacist and technician users, with permissions controlled by admin role settings.
  • Allow users to mark queue items as suggested, verified, preparing, ready, blocked, completed, or skipped.
  • Alert managers or pharmacists about blocked items, overdue work, and workflow failures.
  • Keep source-system clinical verification in the appropriate pharmacy system. AnalyticalRx prepares the queue and tracks operational follow-through.

7.7 Email And Alert Policy

  • In-app alerts are baseline for Phase 2.
  • Internal workflow emails are optional and can be enabled by admins for due work, overdue work, or workflow failures.
  • Doctor outreach emails are optional. Internal scheduling is the default, and outbound sending should be enabled only after product/customer confirmation of templates, sender rules, and approval process.
  • Email drafts or reminders should be preferred before automatic outbound sending.
  • Automatic doctor email sending should be disabled by default.

7.8 How Phase 2 Components Work Together

  1. Phase 1 canonical data, task data, role assignments, and pharmacy/store mappings become the input to Phase 2 workflows.
  2. Predefined automations run on schedules or source events, depending on the workflow.
  3. The automation component creates queue items, tasks, alerts, optional emails, and workflow audit records.
  4. Pharmacists and technicians use the daily refill queue to verify, prepare, block, complete, or skip items.
  5. Business development users use Doctor CRM to schedule visits, review pitch history, capture outcomes, and plan next follow-ups.
  6. Admins control workflow enablement, role access, email settings, and escalation rules.
  7. Role dashboards show the current workflow status: due work, blocked items, overdue follow-ups, automation health, and completion progress.

7.9 Phase 2 Out Of Scope

  • Fully customizable customer-built automation rule engine.
  • Advanced predictive analytics, forecasting, recommendation engine, or scenario planner.
  • Automatic clinical decision-making or medication changes.
  • Unapproved outbound doctor email automation.
  • Broad patient-level CRM inside AnalyticalRx.
  • Replacing PrimeRx as the system of record for prescriptions, clinical verification, or dispensing workflow.
  • External SaaS billing and broad multi-customer commercialization features.

7.10 Phase 2 Acceptance Criteria

  • All Phase 1 role dashboards remain available and are enhanced, not replaced.
  • Admins can grant or restrict workflow access by role, user, and store.
  • Doctor CRM supports office profiles, visit history, pitch history, follow-up scheduling, relationship status, and outcome notes.
  • Business development users can see upcoming doctor visits, follow-ups, overdue outreach, and prior pitch context.
  • The automation component can run predefined scheduled workflows and record workflow audit status.
  • The daily refill workflow prepares today's refill queue and an upcoming refill queue before users manually search for it.
  • Pharmacists and authorized technicians can update refill queue statuses according to role permissions.
  • Automations can create in-app alerts and internal email alerts where enabled.
  • Doctor email sending is disabled by default and can be enabled only through approved admin settings.
  • Workflow failures, skipped records, and missing data are visible to authorized admin/support users.

Appendix B - Product Principles

  • Action over reporting: the product should not only show numbers; it should help users decide what to do next.
  • Phase 1 foundation first: ingestion, canonical storage, audit visibility, and starter dashboards come before advanced analytics.
  • Airtable as reference: Airtable tells us what users are trying to manage, but the SaaS product should not become an Airtable clone.
  • HIPAA-minimized by default: avoid direct patient identifiers in the business analytics layer unless explicitly approved through compliance review.
  • Role-specific visibility: users should only see the stores, dashboards, financials, documents, and workflows required for their role.
  • Predefined workflows before custom rules: Phase 2 should prove the most useful automations before we build a generic rule builder.
  • Explainable insights: future recommendations and projections must show assumptions and supporting data.

Appendix C - Privacy And Security

The product should follow a HIPAA-minimized design. The analytics and dashboard layer should avoid direct patient identifiers wherever possible.

  • Do not display patient names, addresses, dates of birth, phone numbers, emails, SSNs, medical record numbers, prescription images, or other direct patient identifiers in normal dashboards.
  • Prefer aggregated, bucketed, de-identified, or pseudonymized analytics for patient-related insights.
  • Restrict raw payloads and source identifiers to tightly controlled system roles.
  • Use tenant and pharmacy-level access rules so one pharmacy group cannot access another group's data.
  • Maintain import logs, access logs, update logs, workflow audit logs, and document-change logs.
  • Before production PHI/ePHI processing in AWS, confirm AWS BAA coverage and HIPAA-eligible service configuration.

Production readiness note: AWS Artifact BAA agreements are generally available without a separate AWS agreement fee, but normal AWS usage cost, security review effort, and legal/compliance review effort still apply.

Appendix D - Assumptions

  • PrimeRx/Micro Merchant will provide webhook, API, or SFTP access sufficient for Phase 1 ingestion and starter dashboards.
  • Exact PrimeRx/Micro Merchant field availability will be finalized through vendor documentation, API review, and SFTP specification discussions.
  • Some operational data, such as tasks, documents, licenses, doctor visits, and financial period close status, may need to be entered directly into AnalyticalRx or imported from non-PrimeRx sources.
  • Airtable will be used as a reference for product intent, even if the client cancels the Airtable subscription.
  • Phase 1 dashboards will include simple starter views for all roles and improve as source data quality, API access, and historical feeds mature.
  • Phase 2 will start with predefined workflows instead of a fully configurable automation builder.
  • Phase 2 refill queues depend on PrimeRx/Micro Merchant providing enough refill due date, refill status, and prescription continuity data through API, SFTP, webhook, or an approved combination.
  • Doctor email sending will be optional and disabled by default until the product user or customer confirms the desired templates, sender rules, and approval process.
  • Advanced analytics will require enough historical data, consistent mapping, and validated business definitions before client-facing release.
